sections:
  - heading: "A craft of five generations"
    text: "Felipe Linares Vargas models and paints cartonería in Mexico City. He is the son of Felipe Linares Mendoza and the grandson of Pedro Linares López, the cartonero who dreamed of the alebrijes in 1936 and gave them their name. The Linares family worked paper and cardboard before don Pedro, and today describes itself as the fifth generation of the craft."
  - heading: "Each one with their own workshop"
    text: "In the Linares family you learn by helping your father, your grandfather and your uncles; then each member works independently. Felipe Linares Vargas belongs to the generation that grew up among Judas figures for Holy Week, skulls for November and alebrijes all year round. Felipe came home from secondary school and sat beside his father: “help me whiten this, help me decorate that.” He studied programming but chose the workshop. The family credits his father, Felipe Linares Mendoza, with fine work, movement and meticulous painting; in Felipe’s work that lesson is recognizable at once."
  - heading: "The alebrije was born in a dream"
    text: "In 1936, convalescing from a serious illness, Pedro Linares López dreamed of a forest inhabited by colorful creatures (a donkey with wings, a rooster with horns, a lion with a dog’s head) repeating a meaningless word: alebrijes. On recovering, he began to model them in paper and cardboard. He had created a new category within an old craft."
  - heading: "Creatures that gather several animals"
    text: "A cartonería alebrije joins parts of different animals in one body: feline, bird, reptile, amphibian. It is built with paper, cardboard and paste over a light armature, modeled by hand and painted with a brush, spot by spot. In 2019 the Government of Mexico City declared the alebrijes cultural heritage of the city."
  - heading: "Paper, paste and brush"
    text: "Each piece is built from the inside out: a light armature, layers of paper and cardboard glued with paste that dry one by one, the modeling of muzzle, eyes and claws, a base coat and, finally, the hand-painted patterns. It is work of layers and patience."
  - heading: "The works"
    text: "Alebrijes and skeletons modeled in paper and cardboard, with front, side and workshop views. Tap any piece to see it large."
  - heading: "Work in museums and exhibitions"
    text: "The work of Felipe Linares Vargas is part of collections and exhibitions in Mexico and the United States, within a family recognized by the National Prize for Arts and Sciences and by museums in several countries."
