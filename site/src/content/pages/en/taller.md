    text: Layers of paper and cardboard are applied over the armature with paste. Each layer dries before the next, sometimes for days. With hands and simple tools the muzzle, eyes and claws are refined until the surface is continuous.

  - heading: Paint
    text: First a base coat over the whole surface; then the large fields that distinguish each part of the body; finally, spots, scales, veins and dots painted one by one. It is the longest stage and the one that gives each piece its character.

    text: "A final varnish fixes the color and gives the surface its sheen. The creature leaves the workshop complete, with every extension intact: nothing is trimmed to fit a box."

The Linares family works in the Merced Balbuena area, east of Mexico City's historic center, near the Sonora Market, where the houses are workshops at the same time. Each member keeps their own. On these tables, jars of paint, brushes, half-dried pieces and finished figures waiting for their owners all share the space.

The five stations that follow describe the general process of traditional cartonería as documented by the sources consulted and as seen in the workshop photographs on this site. The photographs document the craft in general: they do not show the continuous making of a single piece. The specifics of Felipe Linares Vargas's own method will be added with his confirmation.
